The Enigma machine was a cipher device developed and used in the early- to mid-20th century to protect commercial, diplomatic, and military communication. It was employed extensively by Nazi Germany during World War II.

The Enigma consists of a keyboard, a set of rotating disks (rotors), a plugboard, and a reflector. When a key is pressed, electrical current flows through the components in a complex path, lighting up a different letter on the lampboard.

Polish mathematicians were the first to break Enigma encryption, with their techniques later expanded by British cryptanalysts at Bletchley Park, including Alan Turing. Breaking the Enigma code was crucial to the Allied victory in WWII.
